Pocket Go

close to ship a WWW you inhabit

why you have one

Chat logs are not a place. Folders are not a place. A wiki that forgets which room you were standing in is not a place. If the work is worlds, cases, products, and paper that has to stay related, you need a net you can walk — not another scroll of maybe.

Pocket Go is that net. Hosts are folders. Addresses are go.bigboxco/, go.tags/, go.codes/OT/001. You type a bar. You enter a room. The page is the crate. The thinking has a body.

The dress can look like a fever dream. The machine is not. Markdown on disk. Cabinets that follow the page. BIOS to edit the pocket from inside the pocket.

why markdown like this

Ordinary markdown is a file. Pocket Go markdown is clay in a room. A note can be a hall, a log, a product page, a word, a card. Tokens print lists and holes: files, doors, paper, faces. Wiki links resolve to notes or rooms. The file still opens in any editor. The pocket is how it wears a place, a crate, and a set of mouths.

You store it this way so the material can be walked, filed, cited, and dressed — without becoming a database you cannot hold, and without collapsing into one pile of tags.

BIOS

BIOS is a tiny IDE docked on the desk. Room letter, shell, paper, CSS. Keep. Ctrl+S. Mint a page, a folder, a shell, a host. Pick another host and edit that clay without leaving. The pocket is not a viewer with a secret repo behind it. You change the WWW from inside the WWW.

cabinets

Each cabinet is a different kind of truth about the page you are on. Same crate. Different mouths. Empty on the lobby because the lobby is not filed. Walk into a room.

Librarian

Catalog. Fields on this crate: title, type, energy, whatever label the page actually needs. Booth green. Not tags. A ledger of other pages that share a field.

Charlie

Weave. from * rel > to. Pins, threads, hashes. Amber phosphor. Words that connect pages, not properties that describe them. A hash on the paper is not the same thing as a pin you wrote on the term.

Detective

Hunt. Slips, mindset, fragments with cites. Detective lore on its own tray. Stamp red. Librarian can catalog the same crate and never hold these thoughts.

TPS

When. Named dates: created, referenced, or a title you type. Carbon time-card. Year-only stays a year. It does not pretend to be midnight in January.

Cards

The deck. Lore faces minted from a mouth, edged to crates, hopped. A card is still a crate on the back. The face is the clay you read.

clay tools

Each of these was built for a specific job. They are not one search box wearing five coats.

  • Hosts. A folder becomes go.{name}. Environments dress the room — company intranet, tag shop, chip directory — without being a theme picker on one notes app.
  • Crates. The page is the id. Hop by crate, not by a path that will move. The crate report is every cabinet on that one page.
  • Citations. ^OT-001 or a full chip chain ^OT-008.T01.B002.L03. Using a cite mints the address. Look-through shows what already pointed here.
  • Tags and hashes. Charlie weaves. A #word on the paper is a view of the page. A pin is something you filed. They meet in the same lookup without being the same object.
  • Lore cards. A short face (class, title, line) with crate edges on the back. Attach them to pages. Pop a copy. They are materials, not comments.
  • Wiki. [[Note]] to a file or a room. Stays inside the host you are standing in.
  • Shell and paper. A hall can wear chrome (_shell.md) with a hole for the blotter (index.md). Nested rooms can sit in the parent hole and still have their own crate.

status

Lived-in. Close to ship. The start screen is not the product — it is a lobby I do not love. The software is the rooms, the cabinets, and BIOS. Screenshots of those come next, not a lamp on a destination picker.

Other pocket products, not this one: Pocket Notebook, Pocket Chapbook. Notebook is cloth books. Chapbook is a poem pool. Go is the WWW.
